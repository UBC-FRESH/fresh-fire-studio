Model card · the science behind the studio

Fire is a process, not a perimeter.

FRESH Fire Studio is a deterministic visual replay of the WS4 fire-behaviour stack: FBP surface fire, crown fire, the Canadian FWI System, Huygens growth, terrain, fuel and weather. This page is the model card — what is solved, what is assumed, where it fails, and what we are testing next.

1 · The fire-behaviour kernel

The core is the Canadian Forest Fire Behavior Prediction System, implemented in WS4. Surface fire rate of spread follows the Rothermel form, with the FBP fuel-specific empirical constants and the Canadian slope/wind adjustments. The kernel is not a machine-learned surrogate: every cell is evaluated from fuel, moisture, wind and terrain.

R = (IR · ξ · (1 + φw + φs)) / (ρb · ε · Qig)Rothermel surface rate of spread. IR reaction intensity; ξ propagating flux ratio; φw, φs wind and slope factors; ρb bulk density; ε effective heating number; Qig heat of preignition.

Moisture is carried by the standard FWI System. FFMC controls fine-fuel ignition; DMC and DC carry the slower drought memory; BUI combines them; ISI combines FFMC and wind; FWI combines ISI and BUI. The studio shows the daily codes in each case brief, not a hidden “wetness” slider.

FFMC, DMC, DC → BUI; ISI = f(FFMC, wind); FWI = f(ISI, BUI)Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System. The hourly model advances FFMC from hourly temperature, RH, wind and rain; BUI is held at the event-day value because there is no defensible hourly BUI conversion.

Crown fire follows Van Wagner. Crown initiation needs sufficient surface intensity and crown bulk density; active crown fire needs the critical spread rate. The studio distinguishes surface, passive crown, active crown and post-fire tree outcomes.

I0 = (0.01 · CBH · (460 + 25.9 · FMC))1.5Van Wagner crown initiation intensity, kW/m. CBH = crown base height; FMC = foliar moisture content.
R0 = 3.0 / CBSCritical active-crown rate of spread, m/min. CBS = crown bulk density, kg/m³.

2 · Growth: the Huygens arrival-time field

A fire is not drawn as a polygon. Each cell stores the earliest arrival time of the front. The front advances by propagating an elliptical wave from every burning cell; the perimeter at time t is the envelope of all cells whose arrival time is ≤ t. This is the same Huygens construction used by FARSITE and Prometheus, implemented on a regular grid.

tarrival(j) = mini∈neighbours(j) [ tarrival(i) + dij / R(θij) ]Discrete Huygens propagation. d is cell-centre distance; R(θ) is the directional FBP rate of spread; θ is the direction relative to the effective wind/slope vector.

Wind and slope are folded into an effective wind vector per cell. The model supports heterogeneous fuel type grids, per-cell slope/aspect, conifer/dead-fir blends, crown closure, and a seeded spotting process. Spotting is a screening assumption, not a validated physical ember model.

3 · What the 3D studio actually shows

The 3D layer is an observed-extent replay. The tree and stand geometry is clipped to the mapped final fire perimeter. For CFSDS cases, each tree ignites on the observed day-of-burn raster; for Dogrib and Hay River, arrival time comes from the WS4 growth field inside the observed extent. Trees then resolve to live, burning, scorched, torched or consumed visual states.

This is not a prediction of the square AOI. The default scene is the observed burn shape. The Model extent button overlays the free-burn model footprint in orange, so the difference between observed extent and basin-filling model behaviour is visible.

The tree population is synthetic. Nemora places stems from a species/DBH distribution; it is a plausible realization, not a measured inventory. Tree-scale mortality is a visual classification, not a validated FOFEM output. The 3D scene is a communication and inspection tool; the authoritative output is the gridded arrival-time field and the perimeter metrics.

4 · Validation ladder

We deliberately do not use one fire or one IoU as a pass mark. The ladder has six tiers:

TierQuestionCurrent evidence
Standard casesDoes the kernel reproduce FBP/Rothermel standard cases?Surface, crown, FWI and ellipse benchmarks pass in WS4 tests.
Event reconstructionDoes the simulated perimeter match a public observed fire?Dogrib and Hay River are the depth cases; multi-fire reanalysis cases are reported separately.
Burn probabilityAre probabilities calibrated and discriminative?FSim-style top-20% capture and LSS are the published reference class; P164.3 is paused until a public occurrence dataset is acquired.
Treatment responseDo fuel treatments change risk in the right direction and magnitude?Open for P164.4; containment/firebreak scenarios are now a first-class input.
Tree effectsDo scorch, torch and mortality match FOFEM/Ryan-type evidence?FOFEM ±20% stand-level is the benchmark; the 3D classes are not mortality validation.
Decision qualityDoes uncertainty change the chosen plan?Open for P164.5.

5 · What we found, including the uncomfortable parts

Published event models are modest. Across the WildfireAV/ELMFIRE and FARSITE comparisons, mean event Jaccard is about 0.18; Prometheus Dogrib is 0.21 on the difficult case and 0.568 on the better-conditioned case. Our own six-case reanalysis benchmark is in that range: median IoU 0.096, area bias +6.7× to +11.6×.

We did not hide that. The failures are diagnostic:

  • Point ignition was wrong. Several fires already had a large first-day perimeter. First-perimeter seeding is now supported.
  • Weather was not the culprit. Reanalysis FFMC/ISI were close to CFSDS; terrain and fuel heterogeneity were ruled out by sensitivity runs.
  • The model fills the burnable basin. Across 12 cases, median simulated area was 0.995 of the connected basin while observed area was 0.130. That is a stopping/containment failure, not a hidden ROS multiplier.
  • Small fires are suppression-dominated. Large fires show a much smaller bias — median area bias 2.89×, IoU 0.238 — and residual 100 m bias of 1.4–3.7×. We use large/free-burn cases for ROS evidence and small cases only for containment scenarios.

Every case records its source, access date, licence, grid, event window, weather mode and ignition mode. The data are public; the derived fixtures are small; raw archives stay out of the portal.

7 · Where this is weak

  • Suppression is not solved. Containment masks and firebreaks are scenario inputs; we do not claim to predict suppression.
  • Reanalysis weather is not a station. Small-fire timing is sensitive to local wind and rain.
  • The tree layer is synthetic. The near-field LOD renders a visual 500 stems/ha target from stand geometry; a wider 140 stems/ha tree-silhouette ring carries the background. Neither is an inventory or a mortality sample.
  • Observed-extent replay is not free-burn prediction. The model-extent toggle exists precisely to show that.
  • Burn probability is not resumed. P164.3 needs a public occurrence dataset and a pre-registered skill target.

8 · What we are testing next

  1. Free-burn vs observed toggle as a first-class comparison — show both extents without confusing them.
  2. Containment/firebreak scenarios — a management input, not a tuned parameter.
  3. Treatment-effect validation — direction, magnitude and ranking stability against public experiments.
  4. Tree-mortality evidence — FOFEM/Ryan relationships, with the 3D classes kept separate.
  5. Decision quality — does uncertainty change the plan?
